## Age-appropriate coaching

Hockey Canada U9: 75% technical, 15% individual tactics, 10% team tactics; U11: 50/20/15/10/5 across technical/individual/team/team play/strategy. These are practice allocations, not weights for game grades. USA Hockey ADM favors small-area games, repetitions, puck touches and development. Avoid criticizing 8U players for failing advanced neutral-zone systems. [U9](https://video.hockeycanada.ca/en-ca/hockey-programs/players/essentials/age-divisions/under-9) [U11](https://video.hockeycanada.ca/en-ca/hockey-programs/players/essentials/age-divisions/under-11) [ADM](https://portal.usahockey.com/cx/vice-president/hockey-development/adm_one-sheet.pdf)

## Observable hockey ontology

- Skating: starts, acceleration, stops, edges, transitions, balance; judge only when legs visible.
- Puck skills: reception, carry, protection, handling, shot selection; puck location may be unknown.
- Scanning: head orientation is weak proxy for visual attention; do not assert 'saw teammate' solely from helmet direction.
- Off-puck offense: support triangle, passing lane, width/depth, drive to space, recovery after turnover.
- Defense: inside positioning, gap, stick lane, recovery, containment, backchecking, appropriate nonchecking play.
- Transition: recognize change of possession, immediate first two strides, switch from offense to defense.
- Teamplay: communicate, offer options, avoid unproductive puck-chasing, respect coaches' assigned responsibilities.

## Event decision rubric

For each event record: source timestamps; game phase; visible players and certainty; the selected player identification confidence; objective observations; action taken; at least two plausible options; constraints (pressure, teammates, rules, fatigue, camera occlusion); age-appropriate coaching principle; confidence; what was good; what could improve; suggested practice; positive voiceover.

Evaluate decision quality separately from execution and outcome. A poor result can follow a correct decision; a lucky goal can follow an unwise decision. Avoid counterfactual certainty: 'this route likely opens a lane', not 'this would definitely score'.

## Event selection

Aim 10: four evidence-backed strengths, four learning opportunities, two teachable tactical moments; dynamically adjust if fewer valid examples. Diversity across skills, periods and game situations. Prioritize repeat patterns and clear alternatives over spectacular goals. No more than three actionable practice goals per game.

## Child-facing pedagogy

Parent edition detailed, 6–10 minutes; child edition optional 2–4 minutes with one or two coaching cues. Describe 'next time try...' rather than 'bad'. Keep agency, fun and self-confidence. Celebrate effort and decision-making, not only points.

## Validation and fairness

Cross-check game phase, rules, assigned position, full vs half ice, camera angle and coaching instruction. Use expert-reviewed gold-standard cases. Distinguish visible event from speculation. Flag errors in jersey identity, occlusion, game clock alignment, alternative arrows and claims about gaze.
