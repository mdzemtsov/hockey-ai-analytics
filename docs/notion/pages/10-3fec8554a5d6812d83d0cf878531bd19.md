> Historical Notion snapshot. Design targets and older status claims are not current implementation guarantees. See [current baseline](../../BASELINE_QA.md). Child-identifying names, team name and individual profile details have been generalized for public sharing.

## Source authority

A: USA Hockey 8U/10U practice planners, Hockey Canada U9/U11 pathways and skill matrices, peer-reviewed original youth-sport research; B: publisher-verified coaching books; C: expert opinion and hypotheses. Rule evidence status separate from runtime video certainty.

## Age bands

8U: fundamental skating, puck contact, fun, safe competition, off-puck support as emerging skill; no advanced team systems requirement. 10U: retain fundamentals while increasing individual tactics and basic team play. For a child moving divisions, preserve developmental continuity; no abrupt negative grade inflation.

## Score model — proposal, not validated

For each observable opportunity record three ordinal dimensions 0/1/2/NA: decision appropriateness, technical execution, and result; do NOT sum them into a single player grade. 0 = clear opportunity with clearly inferior choice/execution, 1 = mixed, 2 = age-appropriate useful choice/execution, NA = insufficient view/role context. Each 0 needs at least one plausible alternative grounded in visible evidence and review by qualified youth coach. No psychological scoring.

## Opportunity denominator

Avoid counting only highlights. Record opportunity\_id and eligible observation windows for support, transitions, pass choices, defensive recoveries, shots. Rates use eligible clearly observed opportunities only, with coverage ratio and uncertainty. Compare only similar formats, camera types and game situations. Do not infer absence of skill from absence of opportunity.

## Decision rule schema

rule\_id; version; age\_band; game\_format; situation; positive observable conditions; negative observable conditions; excluded contexts; viable alternatives; video minimum visibility; counterexample; source\_url; page\_or\_section; confidence; coach\_review; suggested drill; child-friendly phrasing; failure tests.

## Initial rules requiring expert review

R01: after turnover, player makes a visible attempt to recover useful defensive position; only assess if change of possession clearly observed.

R02: when teammate carries puck and an unobstructed lane exists, off-puck player offers a useful passing option; avoid rigid lane geometry without rink calibration.

R03: when receiving a pass, player can present stick and keep moving; assess only when pass and skates visible.

R04: when a defender blocks the direct route, puck carrier may protect and change direction; alternative only if space visible.

R05: defensive player protects inside lane without unsafe checking; assess opponent/net geometry and current role.

R06: 1v1 attacker may attempt deke or pass depending on support; do not grade by success alone.

R07: after a failed play, look for observable next action; never infer mental state.

R08: decision between shooting and passing depends on actual open lanes; no hindsight goal bias.

R09: small-area puck pressure encourages quick support; not a blanket rule against carrying puck.

R10: age-appropriate positive effort and learning episodes can be selected even without scoring impact.

## Coverage and validity

No grade on hidden puck, uncertain jersey, uncalibrated geometry for distance-sensitive claims, missing team instructions for role-specific criticism, or inconsistent ruleset. An AI 'uncertain' response is successful quality control, not a failure to be disguised.

## Output to parents

Episode: what was visible → what the selected player did → why it may have helped → alternative if clearly plausible → 1 fun 5-minute practice. End with 2 strengths and at most 2 development themes. Positive/critical ratio is an editorial preference, not a research-established optimal formula.

## Acceptance benchmark

Human gold set: at least 50 varied annotated situations across 8U/10U, including occlusions, similar jerseys, good decision/bad result and bad decision/good result; double-coded by two youth coaches; resolve disagreements and measure agreement before publishing tactical scores. Test identity precision and calibration independently from coaching inference.
