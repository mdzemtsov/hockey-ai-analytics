## A. U9 — age-appropriate constraints (primary federation source)

Source: [2026 Canadian U9 Player Pathway PDF](https://cdn.hockeycanada.ca/hockey-canada/Hockey-Programs/Coaching/u9-program/downloads/2026/u9-player-pathway-e.pdf) and [Hockey Canada U9 age guidance](https://www.hockeycanada.ca/en-ca/hockey-programs/players/essentials/age-divisions/under-9).

**Source statements:** practice emphasis 75% technical skills, 15% individual tactics, 10% team tactics. Fun, fundamental skills and fair/equal ice time are emphasized. Players should rotate through forward, defence and goaltender; basic positional play can be introduced late U9 / before U11. Small-area games and stations are recommended.

**AI inference:** Never penalize the selected player for not maintaining adult-style winger positioning or advanced breakout structure; a 10-episode review should include many skating/puck/support situations. Position rotation and local league rules are explicit context variables. **Do not convert the practice percentages into performance-scoring weights.**

## B. U11 — progressive change, not a discontinuity

Sources: [U11 age guidance](https://video.hockeycanada.ca/en-ca/hockey-programs/players/essentials/age-divisions/under-11), [U11 pathway PDF](https://cdn.hockeycanada.ca/hockey-canada/Hockey-Programs/Coaching/u11-program/downloads/2022/u11-player-pathway-e.pdf), [U11 coaching skill guidance](https://www.hockeycanada.ca/en-ca/hockey-programs/coaching/under-11/coaches/skills).

**Source statements:** 50% technical skills, 20% individual tactics, 15% team tactics, 10% team play, 5% strategy in practice. 85% remains skills/tactics. U11 pathway recommends positional rotation over five games (LW/C/RW/RD/LD) and progressively more consistent positions later.

**AI inference:** When the selected player moves to 10U, gradually add outlet/support, basic defensive shape and entry decisions, but do not abruptly introduce NHL systems as a grading standard. Compare season-over-season only after adjusting for format, opponent level and camera coverage.

## C. Dave Cameron — book-level metadata + original excerpt

Source: [Coaching Hockey With Small-Area Games, Human Kinetics](https://us.humankinetics.com/products/coaching-hockey-with-small-area-games), Dave Cameron, paperback ©2023, ISBN 9781718213791, 200 pp; [publisher excerpt: Creating Games for Younger and Older Athletes](https://us.humankinetics.com/blogs/excerpt/creating-games-for-younger-and-older-athletes).

**Source statements:** publisher describes 50+ small-area games, categorized by offense, defense, transition and special teams; chapter 2 addresses designing games, chapter 8 game-like situations, chapter 9 practice plans. Accessible author excerpt recommends enjoyable games for young players, foundational puck possession/passing, puck support, and moving into available ice.

**AI inference:** Each verified video observation should map to one small-area game exercise rather than a static lecture. Example: the selected player stands behind a defender and is unavailable for a pass → use 2v1 moving support game, not 'memorize the breakout'. This is a proposed mapping; it is not a direct quotation from Cameron.

**Limitation:** publisher description and excerpt are not evidence that every chapter was read. Book is a practical drill resource, not empirical proof of optimal coaching ratios.

## D. Example drill and transfer boundaries

Source: [Human Kinetics excerpt: Crash the Net](https://us.humankinetics.com/blogs/excerpt/a-good-game-for-developing-passing-and-stickhandling-crash-the-net).

**Source statements:** this small-area game develops rebound/net-front plays, loose puck reactions, and finding lanes; publisher describes 10–12 minute activity with multiple players and coaches.

**AI inference:** Map video moments involving rebound pursuit or support near the net to an age-adapted variation. Do not assume the original drill's full setup is suitable for an 8U group without coach review. The user can do a fun 3-minute 'find the rebound' mini-game when appropriate, with safety and facility rules respected.

## E. Five verified observation-to-drill mappings (proposed)

1. Visible turnover → next-play defensive recovery. Event: clear change of possession, the selected player position visible. Coaching: first reaction and recover inside. Drill: continuous 2v2 transition, 3 x 2 min. Exclude hidden puck or absent camera view.
2. Teammate carries with the selected player off puck → become a passing option. Event: full local geometry visible. Coaching: move laterally or diagonally to open lane. Drill: 2v1 support game, 3 x 2 min. Exclude role-specific criticism without team instructions.
3. Receive in stride → soft hands and continue movement. Event: pass and receiver skates visible. Drill: moving pass-and-follow, 3 x 3 min. Do not score if puck invisible.
4. Defender closes puck carrier → protect puck or pass into open support. Event: defender and alternatives visible. Drill: 1v1 with support outlet, 3 x 2 min. Do not infer missed teammate outside frame.
5. Rebound in front → approach loose puck with balance and ready stick. Event: puck and net visible. Drill: simplified net-front scramble, 3 x 2 min. Exclude dangerous body contact.

These doses are design examples, not research-established optimal volumes. Review with a youth coach before recommending as a program.
