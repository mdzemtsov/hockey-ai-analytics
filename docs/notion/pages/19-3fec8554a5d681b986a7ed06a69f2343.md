# 🎯 18 — Training Prescription Engine: decision logic and test cases

[Notion source](https://app.notion.com/p/3fec8554a5d681b986a7ed06a69f2343?pvs=204) · [Index](../README.md)

> Historical Notion snapshot. Design targets and older status claims are not current implementation guarantees. See [current baseline](../../BASELINE_QA.md).

Status: design, not deployed. This engine translates evidence-backed game observations into an optional, age-appropriate micro-training plan.

## Inputs

player\_profile (age band, skill history, enjoyment preferences), game observations (source timestamp, identity confidence, puck visibility, event and rule ID, coach approval), existing schedule (practices, games, other sports, recovery), logistics (ice access, space, gear, parent time), optional fatigue and pain flags.

## Hard safety gates

- Wrong or uncertain player identity → no individualized prescription from that event.
- Uncertain puck/game state → no tactical criticism or skill deficit label.
- Injury/pain, unusual fatigue or distress → do not prescribe additional structured training; advise appropriate rest/evaluation.
- Missing age adaptation or unreviewed exercise → human review before recommendation.
- Parent and child may decline any session; no streaks or punitive scores.
- Existing team schedule always considered; additional ice is not assumed.

## Selection algorithm (conceptual)

1. Ingest verified candidate episodes with source-linked observations.
2. Aggregate eligible opportunities, never just mistakes/highlights. Track camera coverage and unknown states.
3. Identify at most two high-leverage development themes, plus at least one demonstrated strength.
4. Exclude themes supported by only one ambiguous observation.
5. For each theme, match exercise IDs by target rule, developmental age, logistics and safety. Prefer 1 practical off-ice drill and 1 optional coach-integrated on-ice drill.
6. Choose dose: short playful 5–10 min, optional, at a time that does not displace recovery. The dose is a design starting point, not a clinically validated prescription.
7. Generate parent instruction, child-friendly challenge, and coach note. Keep the original video evidence link.
8. Log optional enjoyment and completion, then evaluate similar opportunities in subsequent games with coverage and uncertainty reported.
9. Do not update model weights or claim causal effectiveness from a single successful game.

## Candidate priority function

Use a transparent ordinal rubric, not opaque composite scores:

- evidence quality: confirmed / uncertain;
- repetition: repeated across eligible windows / isolated;
- age relevance: high / moderate / inappropriate;
- coach importance: reviewed / not reviewed;
- feasibility: can do within schedule / requires extra resources;
- child engagement: welcomed / neutral / declined.

Hard gates override all ranks. Display rationale, not a numeric 'talent score'.

## Output contract

plan\_id; game\_id; rule\_version; two\_priorities\_max; evidence\_refs; exercise\_ids; age\_adaptation; duration; instructions; equipment; logistics; optionality; enjoyment; review\_date; coach\_approval; comparison\_opportunities; confidence.

## Test cases

T01 wrong jersey → abstain.

T02 hidden puck → no turnover-based plan.

T03 two visible good passes, one bad reception → do not label 'poor passing'.

T04 child had 4 team practices and tournament → optional rest/free play.

T05 no ice access → off-ice option, not rink booking.

T06 uncertain coach assignment → no role-specific critique.

T07 no opportunities for a skill → no negative grade.

T08 child declines drills → offer unstructured play.

T09 technically poor result after correct decision → focus execution, not decision.

T10 successful goal after poor decision → do not validate decision by outcome.

T11 injury/pain flag → stop extra workload and refer to appropriate professional assessment.

T12 only a single short clip → no longitudinal trend claim.

## Acceptance before production

All output exercises exist in the library, have child-safe instructions and coach review; all personalized claims link to verified video; at least 12 adversarial tests pass; two youth-coach reviewers approve the rubric; no automated release from unreviewed uncertain events.
