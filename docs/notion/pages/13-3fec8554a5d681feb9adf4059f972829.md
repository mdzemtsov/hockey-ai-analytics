# 🧪 12 — Pilot benchmark, annotation handbook and adversarial tests

[Notion source](https://app.notion.com/p/3fec8554a5d681feb9adf4059f972829?pvs=204) · [Index](../README.md)

> Historical Notion snapshot. Design targets and older status claims are not current implementation guarantees. See [current baseline](../../BASELINE_QA.md). Child-identifying names, team name and individual profile details have been generalized for public sharing.

## Goal

Determine whether a human-supervised system can identify the selected player and select defensible teachable moments from real 8U footage, before automation promises. No benchmark numbers are claimed as achieved.

## Sampling

Collect 5–10 minutes of authorized footage first, then three full games from distinct camera angles or venues. Ensure at least 50 human-reviewed candidate situations across skating, puck possession, support, defense and transitions, plus negative cases (wrong jersey, missing puck, occlusion, bench, camera cut). Sample both successful and unsuccessful outcomes to avoid outcome bias. Include no-event intervals for false-positive estimation.

## Annotation protocol

Two youth hockey coaches independently annotate source start/end, player identity, event type, puck visibility, possession change, game state, options, decision, execution, outcome, age appropriateness, uncertainty and suggested drill. They can choose UNKNOWN. Disagreements go to adjudication; preserve both original labels. Parent verifies identity only, not tactical ground truth.

## Metrics

- Identity: precision/recall on visible player segments; count wrong-player episodes separately as critical.
- Tracking: identity switches, lost track time, recovered identity after occlusion.
- Event detection: precision, recall and F1 within a predeclared ±2 s temporal tolerance (proposal; validate).
- Coaching: agreement with adjudicated coaches by situation and severity; fraction of claims fully grounded; hallucination count.
- Coverage: proportion of game time and eligible opportunities where reliable evaluation was possible.
- Media: output clip timestamp error, arrow placement validity, narration factual correctness, audio/video sync, playable MP4.
- Safety: any unconsented publication, diagnosis, unsupported blame or player misidentification is a stop-ship defect.

## Acceptance thresholds — provisional engineering targets, not measured results

Zero wrong-player episodes released; zero unsupported puck-possession claims in narrated clips; all 3 pilot videos playable; 100% of narrated clips linked to source timestamps; two reviewers approve every child-facing critical coaching statement. Set numeric precision/recall gates only after baseline and sample size assessment. No arbitrary '95% accuracy' claim.

## Adversarial cases

1 same jersey number opposing team; 2 two teammates visually similar; 3 player leaves/reenters frame; 4 crowd at boards; 5 net blocks puck; 6 quick camera pan; 7 goal scored after bad decision; 8 perfect pass not received; 9 player out of frame for defensive assignment; 10 coach instructs unusual role; 11 whistle stops play; 12 nonstandard half-ice rules; 13 clip begins mid-possession; 14 low-light/blur; 15 silent footage; 16 replay embedded in recording; 17 camera clocks drift; 18 player jersey changed between games; 19 child distressed/negative framing; 20 unauthorized recording.

## First test readiness checklist

- Parent-approved 5–10 min clip with recording rights.
- Team color, the selected player jersey number, reference still, approximate shifts or timestamps.
- Human annotator available to verify 10 candidate moments.
- Restricted output folder and deletion/retention decision.
- Feasible execution runtime confirmed before ingest.
